## 1. Executive summary

October was the month the website moved from **being indexed** to **being found**. Search
impressions grew more than four-fold inside the month, and the site now appears for 8 classrooms
of demand it did not previously reach: TSS licensing, cloud access, vouchers, shortcuts, company
discovery in CBD Belapur, and Hinglish queries.

The growth, however, did not convert at the same rate. Impressions rose **214%** in the second
half of the month while clicks rose **115%**, and click-through rate fell from **2.37% to 1.25%**.
That is the normal signature of pages entering page one faster than their titles and descriptions
can be tuned for them. The single largest commercial gap is not ranking — it is **capture**.

### Headline numbers

| Metric | October (28 days) |
| :--- | ---: |
| Clicks | **145** |
| Impressions | **8,620** |
| Click-through rate | **1.68%** |
| Average position | **7.03** |
| Pages receiving impressions | **102** |
| Queries logged | **376** |
| Top page (by clicks) | Home — 66 clicks across two URL variants |
| Top page (by impressions) | `/news/how-to-lock-data-in-tallyprime` — 1,647 |
| Best single day | 27 Oct — 13 clicks, 1,038 impressions |

### Five findings that matter

1. **Demand is compounding, capture is lagging.** Impressions ran at 150/day in week 1 and 654/day
   in week 4 (+336%), yet clicks on that same traffic base converted at 1.25% instead of 2.37%.
   The audience arrived; the invitation to click did not keep pace.

2. **One article carries the month.** `/news/how-to-lock-data-in-tallyprime` holds 1,647 impressions
   (19% of all impressions) at an average position of **3.1** and earns 30 clicks (21% of all clicks).
   It is ranked well above its click rate — the clearest, cheapest upside on the site.

3. **The commercial clusters are ranked and invisible.** Tally Cloud access drew 1,171 impressions
   across query and page reports for **one click**. TSS and licence queries drew 378 impressions for
   **zero clicks**, at average positions between 3.5 and 6. Both are revenue audiences, not readers.

4. **Non-brand search does not yet convert.** Branded queries convert at **6.67%**; non-brand at
   **0.82%**. Where non-brand does work — local company discovery in Belapur and Navi Mumbai — it
   converts at **2.29%**, and accounts for 10 of the 19 attributed non-brand clicks. That is the
   proven, repeatable template.

5. **Content production is healthy; content distribution is not.** Fifteen of twenty logged assets
   are marked Ready, two are in design, two await approval, and one is cancelled. Only one row
   carries a posted date — and that row is the tracker's sample entry. The month produced a
   brochure library, not a publishing cadence.

> **The one-line conclusion:** October built reach and collateral. November must convert reach
> into clicks (titles, meta, internal links) and convert collateral into published posts.

#### How the estimates were built

| Position band | Assumed CTR | Basis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 – 2 | 8 – 12% | The snippet is read; the only decision is whether to click |
| 3 – 5 | 4 – 5% | Page one, competitive snippet — copy does most of the work |
| 6 – 8 | 2% | Below the fold on page one |
| 9 – 10 | 1.5% | Highest competition for attention in the snippet |

Each estimate multiplies the page's current impressions by the target CTR for its band, subtracts
clicks already earned, and assumes impressions do not change. That is deliberately a floor rather
than a forecast: the 29 October title deployment is expected to lift several of these at once, and
no estimate assumes a single new page or a single new impression.

### 7.1 The four that deserve a decision this month

**1. `/news/how-to-lock-data-in-tallyprime` — the single highest-return edit on the site.**
1,647 impressions, position 3.11, 1.82% CTR. At position 3, a page normally earns 8–12%; this
one earns 1.8%. The gap is almost entirely title and description intent — the query family
("how to lock tally data for previous year", "how to lock financial year in tally prime",
"how to lock period in tally prime", "how to lock back date entry in tally prime") is
answered by the page but not *promised* by the snippet. A title that states the action, the
version and the outcome should move this page from 30 clicks toward 70–80.

**2. Tally Cloud access — a commercial product page at position 4.12 with zero clicks.**
`/cloud/tallycloudaccess` holds 298 impressions at position 4.12 and `/news/tally-cloud-access`
holds 462 at 10.69, while the query `tallycloudaccess` alone pulls 411 at 3.63. Three URLs,
one intent, one click. This is the site's most visible commercial asset doing nothing —
and the intent behind it (a business looking for Tally on Cloud) is exactly the audience that
buys hosting.

**3. TSS and licence renewal — ranked, revenue-relevant, and uncaptured.**
Forty queries, 378 impressions, zero clicks, average positions 3.5–6 on the strongest entries
("tss expired in tally means", "tss full form in tally", "tss verify", "tss has expired in tally").
The people searching this are mid-problem with a lapsed licence. They are the closest thing to
an inbound renewal lead the site can get, and today they land on a page that does not ask them
to act.

**4. Local company discovery — the proven template, underexploited.**
"it companies in belapur", "belapur it company list", "software companies in navi mumbai",
"tally partner near me", "tally certified partner near me", "list of it companies near me":
48 queries, 436 impressions, **10 clicks at 2.29%** — 53% of all attributed non-brand clicks from
7% of non-brand impressions. Only two pages currently serve this intent. A structured set of
local pages (Belapur, Navi Mumbai, CBD Belapur services, Tally partner in Mumbai, careers in
Belapur) is the most reliable lead-generation move available.

### 12.3 Lessons

1. **Ranking and converting are different jobs, and the site is now good at the first one.**
   Eight page-one pages produce zero clicks. Ranking work is largely done for the clusters that
   matter; the binding constraint has moved to copy and structure.

2. **Two content types convert and both are cheap.** Local discovery and how-to locking queries
   produce 17 of 19 non-brand clicks. Both are informational, both are answerable without
   proprietary data, and both already exist in some form. Doubling them is low-risk.

3. **The Tally Insights investment is paying back early.** A section built in the last ten days of
   the month already holds 22.6% of impressions. Structure — individually indexable pages — is
   what converted a curriculum into a search asset. The same treatment applied to `/products`,
   `/cloud` and `/services` is the obvious next deployment.

4. **Commerce is invisible in search.** `/shop`, `/addons`, `/products` and `/hrms` together hold
   245 impressions. The site sells licences, renewals and hosting; search does not yet know that.
   Product-page SEO is effectively unstarted, and it is the shortest path from traffic to revenue.

5. **Design capacity is being spent where search cannot see it.** 40% of October's content output
   was brochure and catalogue work; 5% was social. The pages winning impressions are editorial.
   A modest reallocation toward search-facing writing would compound far faster than more
   brochures.

6. **The brand SERP has an identity problem, not a ranking problem.** The brand's own term draws
   252 impressions at position 5.87 with zero clicks while a dozen lookalike entities occupy the
   same query space. Metadata will not fix this; entity consistency will.

## 14. Measurement notes

**Data definitions.**

- *Clicks* — Google Search result clicks, not on-site sessions. Excludes paid, direct and social.
- *Impressions* — a result link view on a search results page. Not unique visitors.
- *CTR* — clicks ÷ impressions. *Position* — impression-weighted average.
- *Brand query* — any query starting with the Sarvadnya name or its recorded spelling variants
  (`sarvadnya`, `sarvadnya`, `sarwadnya`, `sarvadnya`, `sarvodaya`).
- *Theme cluster* — a keyword family applied to the non-brand query set; one query belongs to the
  first matching cluster, so clusters do not double-count.

**Caveats.**

1. The Search Console export covers **1–28 October** (last 28 days, web search only). Site-side
   reporting covers 1–31 October. The two windows differ by three days.
2. The query table itemises **44 clicks and 2,684 impressions** — 30% of clicks and 31% of
   impressions. The remainder sits in long-tail queries not present in the file. Brand/non-brand
   and cluster analysis therefore describe the visible third.
3. Page-level sums (146 clicks / 9,708 impressions) exceed the site totals by 1 click and 1,088
   impressions because duplicate URL variants (`http://`, `www`) are reported as separate pages.
4. This is the first month in the reporting cycle with a full Search Console export. No
   month-on-month search comparison is presented; the comparisons above are intra-month.
5. The 29 October titles/descriptions release is **outside** the data window. Its effect is not
   represented anywhere in this report and will first appear in the November export.
6. The tracker's dashboard tiles count the yellow sample rows (WEB-001, DC-001) and were not
   recalculated for this export; row-level figures are used throughout this report.
