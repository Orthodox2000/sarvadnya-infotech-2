### §1 - Why the metadata was rewritten at all

The pages already ranked and already had descriptions - and still earned very few clicks. A title that
**names the page** ("TallyPrime Gold") competes with a dozen identical titles; a title that **states the
offer** ("Buy TallyPrime Gold - Multi-User") tells a searcher the result is the thing they wanted.
That is the whole rationale, and it is why the rewrite is verb-led rather than keyword-stuffed.

43 pages rewritten. Legal and support surfaces (`/privacy`, `/terms`, `/eula`) deliberately keep plain
descriptive titles - a sales verb on a policy page reads wrong and erodes trust exactly where a visitor
is checking whether we are legitimate.

### §2 - `seoMetadata()` gained `contactSuffix` (default `true`)

Every page description carried the same phone/location tail. That is worth its characters on a
commercial page (it answers "are you reachable / are you near me" in the SERP), and it is a waste on a
reference surface, where those characters should carry the actual answer.

- `contactSuffix: false` on **17 call sites**: `learn-sara/[topic]`, tally-insights `[section]` and
  `[section]/[lesson]`, `news/[slug]`, `/news`, `/learn-sara`, `/tally-insights`, `/tools`,
  `/tools/tss-expiry-checker`, `/privacy`, `/terms`, `/eula`, `/search`, `/ask-sara`,
  `/theme-settings`, `learn-sara/chat`.
- Rendered reality today: **32 pages keep the CTA tail, 13 opt out** (the audit prints this on every
  run, so the decision stays visible instead of becoming folklore).

`scripts/seo-audit.mjs` is suffix-aware per call, so the 160-character limit is measured against what
actually renders. Without that, an opted-out page would be checked against a longer string than it
ships and the guard would go quietly wrong.

### §3 - `localBusinessJsonLd()`: three additions, two deliberate omissions

Added: `sameAs` (the four verified socials already present in `orgJsonLd`), `foundingDate: 2008`, and
`hasMap`.

**Not added: `geo` (lat/lng) and `openingHoursSpecification`.** We have no verified coordinates and no
verified opening hours. A guessed pin sends a customer to the wrong building and guessed hours create a
support call at the wrong time - both are worse than the omission, and Google treats neither as an
error. If the owner supplies the real values, they are a two-line addition.

### §5 - Build fix found while validating: a client island pulled `node:crypto` into the browser

`app/(site)/shop/shop-browser.tsx` imported `isGatewayConfigured` from `@/lib/cart/payment`. That
module pulls `node:crypto` and the Razorpay SDK, so `next build` failed with
`UnhandledSchemeError: Reading from node:crypto`. It could never have worked at runtime either: the
function reads `RAZORPAY_KEY_ID`, not a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variable, so in a browser it is always `false`.
Static **"Get Quote"** restored. The owner's "Best Seller" badge repositioning was kept.

This is the class of defect worth remembering: a server helper is not automatically usable from a
client component, and the failure only appears at build time.

### §8 - The chat problem: prove it, then fix it

Prompt rules alone had already failed once (the TDL guard, §28). Twice more they failed here. So the
approach was: **build a behavioural battery first**, and only fix what it can demonstrate.

`/tmp/opencode/verify-chat-quality.mjs` - **22 cases** against a local production build
(`next start -p 3311`), grouped:

| Group | Cases | What it must prove |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A casual | `hi`, `how r u`, `what?` | warm and human, **not** a catalogue dump |
| B gibberish | `i dont ge tit`, `asdkjhqw` | asks, does not guess |
| C product | TSS, Gold price, duplicate-purchase add-on, TallyDrive | facts are real |
| D business problem | outstanding payments, stock across 3 godowns | real product **and** a question |
| E code trap | `can you draft tdl` (x2), `write me a python script` | no code, ever |
| F off-topic | prime minister, poem about rain | decline gracefully, **no pitch** |
| G TSS facts | when to renew, 2-year discount, ERP 9 | no invented windows/discounts/bonus months |
| H hinglish | `bhai tally ka price kya hai` | an answer, not a crash |
| I learn mode | company creation, `write a tdl for me` | teaches, does not write code |
| J multi-turn | renew TSS -> "how do i do that offline" | the second answer uses the first turn |

Assertions cover only **hard** rules (code markers, a product button on an off-topic answer, a
percentage on a discount answer, duplicate CTAs). Judgement quality is printed raw for a human to read,
because "is this a good answer" is not something a regex should pretend to settle.

### §9 - Four defects the battery found, and how each was closed

**(I2) The same question and the same button, twice.** The TDL reply carried the discovery question and
the CTA button, and the guard then appended a second block with both, because `hasDiscoveryQuestion()`
counted only a literal `?` - and the reply asked "Tell me what you are trying to achieve on your
screen", which is a question without a question mark. Fixed by widening the definition
(`INFO_REQUEST_RE`: *tell me*, *let me know*, *share*, *describe*, *which screen/version/edition*,
*how many users/companies/godowns*...), and by `hasCtaButton()` so an appended block never repeats a
button the reply already has.

**(F1/F2) An off-topic answer ended with a product button.** "Who is the prime minister of india" ->
*"That would be Narendra Modi."* + **Explore TallyInsights**; a poem about rain -> **Explore
TallyDrive**. Nothing kills a conversation faster than a poem followed by an advert. Fixed in code:
`isOffTopicQuery()` + `stripCtaButtons()`, so a purely off-topic answer **keeps its words and loses the
button**. A `BUSINESS_CONTEXT_RE` override keeps genuine questions safe - *"how do i show capital of
the firm in the balance sheet"* contains the off-topic token "capital of" but is an accounting
question, and stripping its CTA would cost a real lead.

**(G1) An invented TSS lead time.** The answer said to renew "a few days before expiry"/"15 days
before" - a window Tally does not publish (removed from the knowledge base in §31). The prompt now bans
**every** lead-time window (not just the number 15) and states the correct WHEN wording: renew when
`Alt+R` appears, i.e. when renewal is due. Post-fix reply: *"You should renew your TSS before it
expires... Gateway of Tally > F1: Help > Settings > License > Manage License"*.

**(G2) A discount we do deny and do not deny.** Asked *"is there a 10% discount if i pay for 2 years"*,
Sara answered *"We do not offer a flat 10% multi-year discount"* - **while our own `/services/tss`
table shows `10% OFF` on the 2-year rows**. `lib/prices-catalog.mjs` carries `discountRupees` 1062 /
3186 and `discountLabel: '10% OFF'` for `tss-single-2yr` / `tss-multi-2yr`, rendered 6x on the live
page. A later run had the model **denying** it, so the answer is now enforced in code and is
**neutral**: point at the live pricing table, neither promise nor deny, never state a percentage
(`buildDiscountReply`, action `pricing-redirect`). A 2-year term exists only for TSS on our list, so a
"pay for 2 years" question routes to TSS pricing even when the word TSS is absent; the generic branch
points at `/products`. A custom-build request is excluded - "discount on my TDL project" belongs in the
brief, not a price list.

**(D2) A self-contradicting edition.** "Stock across 3 godowns" was answered with Gold - correct for
multi-user, but the reply also implied a single-user assumption. The prompt gained an **EDITION LOGIC**
block (Silver = single user, Gold = multi-user, Server = client-server) with the instruction to ask the
user count **first**. Post-fix reply asks *"How many people handle your inventory entries across these
locations?"* and recommends Gold.

### §10 - Why this had to be code, not another prompt bullet

Every one of the four defects was already against a written prompt rule. Two of them (F1's button, G2's
denial) survived a **prompt-only** fix and only changed once a deterministic gate was added.

> A prompt is a request. A gate is enforcement.

That is the same reasoning as `lib/sara-guard.mjs` §28: the model can drift, the gate cannot, and the
rule becomes testable **without an LLM**. The guard is applied to every reply in **both** provider
branches before it leaves the server, and logs `[chat] consultant guard: <action>`.

`applyConsultantGuard()` actions are now:
`none` | `blocked-code` | `added-discovery` | `soft-length-nudge` | `pricing-redirect` |
`stripped-offtopic-cta`.

### §11 - Guard suite 51 -> 68 assertions, negative-controlled

`scripts/sara-guard-test.mjs` grew from **51** to **68** assertions: the new prompt policies (off-topic
answers carry no button; the learn prompt's forced tie-back is softened so an honest answer can leave
the pitch out; the TSS block bans every window and states the WHEN wording; discounts are neither
promised nor denied), guard behaviour pairs (off-topic vs business-context, discount vs TSS-vs-generic
branch, TDL request excluded from the discount redirect) and the **exact live replies that regressed**
are pinned as inputs with their required outputs.

Negative controls were run, not assumed: reverting the `INFO_REQUEST_RE` widening fails the two I2
assertions; disabling the off-topic strip fails F1; restoring both passes. A guard that has never been
seen to fail is not a guard.

`scripts/sara-topics-test.mjs` was **not** weakened - its TSS-facts section (~lines 156-196) and its
"no `10 %` in canned answers" rule stay intact, because the canned answers must stay neutral for the
same reason the AI replies now are.
